2.1 · How alumina is made — the whole process

The Bayer Process

Two of the four steps carry nearly all the emission

Step 1 of 4

Digestion

150–270 °C70% of refinery emissions

Bauxite meets hot caustic soda under pressure — and the alumina dissolves out of the ore.

  • Bauxite is finely ground, then mixed with caustic soda and steam in digester vessels at 150–270 °C and high pressure.
  • The alumina content dissolves, forming a sodium aluminate liquor.
Step 2 of 4

Clarification

low temperatureminor energy use

Everything in the ore that never dissolved is settled out and removed.

  • The liquor flows into settling tanks where the undissolved impurities sink and are separated from the sodium aluminate solution.
  • That residue is bauxite residue — "red mud" — the industry's largest waste stream.
Step 3 of 4

Precipitation

low temperatureopen-top tanks

Cooled and seeded, the liquor precipitates alumina as crystals.

  • The clarified liquor is cooled and seeded with fine crystals to start the reaction.
  • The precipitated product — called hydrate — is washed and passed to the final stage.
Step 4 of 4

Calcination

> 1,000 °C24% of refinery emissions

Above 1,000 °C the bound water is driven off — and pure alumina remains.

  • Hydrate is calcined at temperatures exceeding 1,000 °C, driving off chemically bound water.
  • What is left is anhydrous alumina (Al₂O₃) — the refinery's product, shipped to smelters.
  • That heat comes from burning gas or coal, so the resulting CO₂ is direct, on-site emissions.
Emission — ~ 0.72 CO2-e per tonne of alumina, today mostly gas & coal

2.1 · Where each stage can go

Two enablers: green electricity & green hydrogen

Digestion — 70% of emissions → electricity

  • Electric boilers replace gas-fired steam — up to −71% of digestion emissions with firm renewables.
  • MVR recompresses waste vapour — potential ~−70% of the refinery footprint.

Needs: a firmed, low-cost national green grid.

Calcination — 24% of emissions → hydrogen

  • Hydrogen calcination — >1,000 °C heat is hard to electrify.

Needs: a green-hydrogen supply chain at scale.

The Safeguard Mechanism

Australia's compliance scheme for industrial emitters — and how it sets the bar for alumina refineries.

ENGY 7112 · Oral Presentation8 / 22
2.2 · What is SGM?

A declining emissions cap on each large facility

  • Applies to facilities emitting > 100 kt CO₂-e of scope-1 emissions a year — including every Australian alumina refinery.
  • Each facility gets an annual baseline (an emissions limit). Since the July 2023 reform, baselines decline 4.9% every year to 2030.
  • Emit below the baseline → earn Safeguard Mechanism Credits (SMCs). Emit above it → must buy and surrender SMCs / ACCUs.
  • Trade-exposed facilities can apply for a discounted decline rate (TEBA) so the carbon cost doesn't just push production offshore. (QAL and Yarwun received TEBA with 1% decline rate until 2026)

Need electricity & hydrogen

3.2 · Model restrictions

The boundary of this model

1

Aggregate Safeguard treatment

  • One 10.463 Mt reference for all five sites, so site gains and losses are netted
  • Uniform TEBA is a counterfactual; individual determinations, banking and borrowing are omitted
2

Energy proxies, not actual refinery supply

  • NEM capacity, SWIS renewable share and national H₂ output are projected trajectories from planning documents
  • They track national build-out, not the power and hydrogen actually delivered to each refinery
Also outside scope
3
Equipment is assumed readyTechnology, construction and commissioning lead times are not modelled
4
Output held at FY2024-25Production, closures and ownership stay fixed to 2050
5
Scope 1 compliance view onlyElectrified load moves emissions to the grid (Scope 2)

Conclusion

What the modelling and the commitments show.

ENGY 7112 · Oral Presentation20 / 22
5 · Conclusion
  • Refineries meet their limit only if clean power and hydrogen arrive on time
    With electricity and hydrogen delivered on schedule (Good), the five refineries stay below the standard Safeguard baseline from 2028 to 2048 and earn a net A$0.71bn in credits. With a five-year delay (Delayed), they sit above it in every year from 2026 to 2050.
  • A five-year delay would cost the refineries about A$4.1 billion
    Delayed against the standard baseline means about 54 Mt of surrenders over 2026–50: A$4.11bn at EY central prices (A$7.5bn at the cap), averaging 11.7% of the refineries' EBIT and reaching 27.2% in 2050 — above the 3% TEBA threshold in every year from 2028.
  • All aim for net zero by 2050, but little has been delivered so far
    All five refineries sit under net-zero-2050 group targets, yet the only delivered abatement is Worsley's coal-to-gas switch (first boiler −208 kt/yr, about 2% of their emissions). QAL's double digestion is still at feasibility with no investment decision, Wagerup's MVR was discontinued and calcination is still at pilot scale — while WA has no climate law and only a policy target for renewables, and Queensland repealed its renewable targets.
